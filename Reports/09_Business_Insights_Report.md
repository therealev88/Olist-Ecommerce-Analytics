# Phase 9 — Business Insights Report

## 1. Objective

Phase 9 translated the project's analytical findings into business-focused conclusions while avoiding unnecessary repetition of Phases 7 and 8.

## 2. Business Questions

1. What actually drove marketplace growth?
2. Which categories drive the greatest commercial value, and how concentrated is that value?
3. How dependent is the marketplace on acquiring new customers versus retaining existing ones?
4. How valuable are the marketplace's repeat and high-value customers?
5. What is the relationship between delivery performance and customer satisfaction?
6. Which categories combine delivery problems with weaker customer satisfaction?
7. How concentrated is marketplace value among sellers, and does seller scale indicate performance?
8. Are interstate transactions more commercially valuable than in-state transactions?

## 3. 9.1 — Marketplace Growth

Early 2017 versus early 2018: average monthly orders increased from 1,754 to 7,069.33 (+303.0%); average monthly payment value increased from R$293,419.88 to R$1,089,039.88 (+271.3%); average order value fell from R$168.28 to R$153.91 (-8.5%).

**Business insight:** Marketplace growth was overwhelmingly volume-driven rather than basket-size-driven.

## 4. 9.2 — Category Value Concentration

- **Top 5 categories:** R$6,204,400.28, representing **39.16%** of item value.
- **Top 10 categories:** **62.32%** of item value cumulatively.

**Business insight:** The catalogue is broad, but commercial value is highly concentrated among leading categories.

## 5. 9.3 — Customer Acquisition vs Retention

| Customer group | Customers | Customer share | Orders | Order share | Payment value | Value share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| One-time | 93,098 | 96.88% | 93,098 | 93.62% | R$15.06M | 94.10% |
| Repeat | 2,997 | 3.12% | 6,342 | 6.38% | R$944K | 5.90% |

**Business insight:** The marketplace is predominantly acquisition-driven. Repeat customers are more active than their customer share suggests, but remain too small a segment to materially reduce dependence on one-time customers.

This does not prove that retention is poor because customers who did not return may simply fall outside the observation period.

## 6. 9.4 — Repeat Customer Value

| Customer group | Customers | Avg orders/customer | Avg customer value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One-time | 93,098 | 1.00 | R$161.82 |
| Repeat | 2,997 | 2.12 | R$314.99 |

Repeat customers generate approximately 94.7% higher average customer value than one-time customers.

**Business insight:** Repeat customers are a small segment with substantially higher individual commercial value, making retention a potentially valuable growth lever.

## 7. 9.5 — Delivery Performance vs Customer Satisfaction

| Delivery status | Reviewed orders | Avg review score | Negative reviews |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| On Time | 88,652 | 4.29 | 9.23% |
| Late | 7,700 | 2.57 | 54.03% |

Late deliveries are associated with a 1.72-point lower average review score and a negative-review rate nearly six times higher than on-time deliveries.

**Business insight:** Delivery reliability is a major customer-experience factor. This establishes association, not causation.

## 8. 9.6 — Category Delivery & Satisfaction

Among categories with at least 500 reviews, office furniture stood out with a 3.52 average review score and a 9.00% late-delivery rate across 1,246 reviews.

Other categories with weaker satisfaction and elevated late rates included bed_bath_table, furniture_decor, telephony, baby, health_beauty, and auto.

Electronics provided an important counterexample: it had a 9.81% late rate but a 4.07 average review score.

**Business insight:** Office furniture is the clearest category requiring operational attention, but category satisfaction cannot be explained by delivery performance alone.

## 9. 9.7 — Seller Concentration & Scale

| Seller group | Item value | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Top 10 sellers | R$2.03M | 12.80% |
| Sellers 11–50 | R$3.08M | 19.44% |
| Sellers 51–100 | R$1.92M | 12.13% |
| Remaining sellers | R$8.81M | 55.63% |

The top 100 sellers generated 44.37% of item value.

Seller volume also increased sharply with average seller value: Q1 averaged R$384.31 versus R$19,575.76 in Q5.

**Business insight:** Marketplace value is distributed across a broad seller base, while higher seller volume is strongly associated with higher commercial value. This is an association, not proof that volume causes value.

## 10. 9.8 — Interstate vs In-State Commercial Value

| Trade type | Orders | Order share | Item value | Value share | Avg order value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| In-State | 35,600 | 35.89% | R$4.80M | 30.28% | R$134.76 |
| Interstate | 63,583 | 64.11% | R$11.05M | 69.72% | R$173.72 |

Interstate orders have an average item value approximately 28.9% higher than in-state orders.

**Business insight:** Interstate commerce is both the majority of marketplace activity and disproportionately valuable.

## 11. Phase 9 Summary

1. Marketplace growth was primarily volume-driven.
2. Commercial value is concentrated in the leading categories.
3. The marketplace is predominantly acquisition-driven.
4. Repeat customers are few but substantially more valuable individually.
5. Late delivery is strongly associated with poor customer satisfaction.
6. Office furniture is the clearest combined delivery and satisfaction concern.
7. Seller value is broadly distributed, while seller scale is strongly associated with value.
8. Interstate commerce is disproportionately valuable.

## 12. Conclusion

Phase 9 converted the project's descriptive and advanced analytical work into business conclusions without unnecessarily repeating earlier phases.

**Phase 9 is complete.**
